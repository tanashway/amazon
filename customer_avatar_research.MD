# Customer Avatar Research

## Main Insight

This research is built around one practical idea:

Marketing gets stronger when we deeply understand the specific customer, their current state, the desired future state, and the transformation they are trying to buy.

For our baking brand, this means we should not only describe a sourdough kit as a collection of parts. We need to define who is buying it, what they feel before buying, what they want after buying, and what purchase drivers make them choose our kit over another one.

## Customer Avatar Canvas Fields

The Customer Avatar Canvas includes:

- Avatar/persona.
- Demographics and interests.
- Key purchase drivers.
- Frustrations and fears.
- Wants and aspirations.
- Before state.
- After state.

The Before and After sections are broken into:

- Have.
- Feel.
- Average day.
- Status.
- Good vs. evil.

## How To Use This For Our Baking Brand

Each major customer avatar should be filled out as a separate canvas. Do not try to force every buyer into one generic profile.

Recommended priority avatars:

1. Beginner sourdough baker.
2. Gift buyer.
3. Family/home activity baker.
4. Creative baker.
5. Health-conscious homemade bread buyer.

Secondary avatars:

- Eco-conscious baker.
- Experienced artisan home baker.
- Busy home cook.

## Beginner Sourdough Baker Avatar

### Before

Have:

- Interest in sourdough but not a clear process.
- Confusing videos, recipes, and tool lists.
- Fear of buying the wrong supplies.
- Maybe a starter jar but no full workflow.

Feel:

- Curious but intimidated.
- Unsure whether sourdough is too difficult.
- Worried about wasting time and ingredients.

Average day:

- Sees sourdough content online.
- Saves recipes but delays starting.
- Reads reviews to understand which kit is actually complete.

Status:

- Beginner.
- Wants to feel capable and proud.

Good vs. evil:

- Good: clear, guided, approachable baking.
- Evil: confusing, elitist, overcomplicated sourdough advice.

### After

Have:

- Tools for starter care, mixing, shaping, proofing, scoring, baking transfer, and cleanup.
- A clear sense of what each component does.
- A better first baking experience.

Feel:

- Confident enough to start.
- Proud of making a real loaf.
- More connected to the craft.

Average day:

- Feeds starter.
- Uses the jar markings/tracker.
- Mixes and proofs with the right tools.
- Bakes and shares the result.

Status:

- From confused beginner to capable home baker.

Good vs. evil:

- Good: guided progress and visible results.
- Evil: incomplete kits, missing instructions, fragile tools, and vague claims.

## Gift Buyer Avatar

### Before

Have:

- Need for a thoughtful baking gift.
- Concern that a partial kit will feel cheap.
- Limited time to research every tool.

Feel:

- Wants the gift to look premium and complete.
- Worried about giving something confusing or low quality.

Key purchase drivers:

- Gift-ready packaging.
- Complete kit presentation.
- Clear contents.
- Strong perceived value.
- Good reviews and product photos.

### After

Have:

- A ready-to-gift baking experience.
- A box that feels complete without needing extra shopping.

Feel:

- Confident the recipient will understand and enjoy the gift.
- Proud to give something useful, beautiful, and personal.

## Core Purchase Drivers For The Listing

The customer avatar work points to these high-priority purchase drivers:

- Complete kit, not random bundle.
- Beginner-friendly guidance.
- Clear component labels and visual explanation.
- Durable jar and protected packaging.
- Natural rattan baskets and washable liners.
- Gift-ready box.
- Honest claims.
- Easy cleanup and storage.
- Useful tools for every major sourdough step.

## Frustrations And Fears To Address

Use listing images, bullets, A+ content, insert cards, and support docs to reduce these fears:

- "Will I know what to do with all these tools?"
- "Is this actually complete?"
- "Will the glass jar arrive broken?"
- "Are the baskets smooth and clean?"
- "Is this beginner-friendly or only for experts?"
- "Will this be nice enough as a gift?"
- "Are there useless filler pieces?"
- "Will the instructions be clear?"

## Wants And Aspirations To Speak To

Customers want:

- To bake beautiful bread at home.
- To feel capable instead of confused.
- To create something handmade and shareable.
- To make a gift feel thoughtful.
- To control ingredients.
- To participate in a craft that feels wholesome and rewarding.

## Core Message Canvas Implication

The Core Message Canvas connects:

- Before state.
- Avatar/persona.
- Product/service.
- After state.
- Statement of value.
- Metaphor.
- Jobs to be done.

For our sourdough kit:

Product/service:

Complete guided sourdough baking kit.

Statement of value:

Give home bakers the tools and guidance to start sourdough with more confidence, from starter care to a finished loaf.

Metaphor:

A guided baking station in a box.

Jobs to be done:

- Start or maintain a sourdough starter.
- Mix dough with less mess.
- Shape and proof round and oval loaves.
- Score safely and decoratively.
- Transfer dough into hot bakeware.
- Clean and store tools.
- Gift or share the finished bread.

## Listing Guidance

The listing should show the transformation, not only the component count.

Recommended image sequence:

1. Complete kit hero.
2. What each piece does.
3. Starter care and jar tracking.
4. Mix, shape, and proof workflow.
5. Score, bake, and transfer workflow.
6. Beginner-friendly guide and QR/video support.
7. Gift-ready packaging.
8. Quality and safety details.

## Team Takeaway

The main customer insight is not simply "people want a sourdough kit."

The stronger insight is:

Customers want to become the kind of person who can make beautiful homemade bread, but they need the process to feel clear, complete, and low-risk.

Every product, listing, image, insert, and brand message should reduce confusion and increase confidence.
